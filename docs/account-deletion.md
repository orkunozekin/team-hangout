# Account deletion

Step 6 keeps `deleteAccount` and `{ ok: true, membershipsRemoved }`. A pending cleanup adds `jobStatus: pending`; the existing client signs out after acceptance. Initial requests require an Auth `auth_time` within five minutes. Sign out and sign in again when prompted. Retries of an accepted job do not require fresh authentication. Completed retries return the legacy zero-count response.

The first transaction creates a server-only `accountDeletionJobs/{uid}` tombstone and marks the profile deleting. Rules and every callable entry point reject that identity immediately, including cached tokens, command replays and accidentally recreated profiles. Assignment commands also reject deleting targets. Do not delete the tombstone or reuse its UID. It contains status, phase, cursor, timestamps, counters and a redacted retry code, never email addresses or token values. Deletion is irreversible once accepted.

`resumeAccountDeletions` runs every five minutes under its service identity, selecting ten pending jobs ordered by last attempt. It processes at most 30 pages per job; each page has at most 25 records. Firestore transactions commit cleanup, cursor and counts together, so interrupted and overlapping workers safely resume. Auth deletion happens after Firestore cleanup. Missing Auth users count as success; provider failure leaves the job pending and denied. A crash after provider deletion resumes without caller authentication. The callable also attempts 30 pages, preserving quick completion for small accounts.

Cleanup removes team grants, organization staff grants, guardian grants, private user projections, command receipts, owner bootstrap grants and the Auth/profile records. Existing organization member records have no userId, so the worker scans the `members` collection group in bounded pages and verifies canonical paths and document IDs. This is a full scan, with a durable path cursor. Guardians use canonical `userId`; self-linked participants use `linkedUserId`. Self unlinking increments participant/access revisions and removes that participant's account contact email/phone. Other guardians' grants and discovery sources remain intact. Participant identities, rosters, teams, events and organization operational records remain. Organization-owned private fields and unstructured guardian notes cannot be attributed to a particular account; their retention/redaction policy remains a launch gate.

Removing the last active administrator sets `administrativeControl: recoveryRequired` and creates a private audit event. It never grants a replacement account authority or blocks deletion. The survivor check considers at most 100 admin candidates and checks their tombstones; unusually large administrator sets can conservatively require support review. Removing the only active guardian sets `guardianRecoveryRequired`; it does not delete the child. Recovery flags are support indicators, not grants or automatic organization archival. Current canonical grants still authorize surviving admins. There is no public recovery callable: support must separately verify identity/organization ownership, record an audit and explicitly assign a replacement through a trusted server procedure. Never assign the first person to claim an orphaned organization. Staff handover before deletion is preferred.

## Hooks for later steps

`cleanupHooks` declares server-only top-level collection ownership contracts: `calendarSubscriptions.ownerUid`, `invitations.recipientUid`, `emailOutbox.recipientUid`, `notificationEndpoints.userId`, and `commandRequests.uid`. Matching rows are deleted. Account-private `organizationLinks`, `participantLinks`, `teamAccess`, `agenda`, `notificationEndpoints` and `deviceTokens` subcollections are paged too. Future modules must use these contracts, check the deletion tombstone before issuing grants/tokens/projections or performing feed reads/sends, and transactionally fence creation against deletion. New account-owned collections or nested records require explicit cleanup stages and regression fixtures. No feed, invitation lifecycle or email transport is implemented in this step. Already copied emails/calendar entries cannot be recalled.

## Operations and rollout

Deploy the checked-in indexes, callable/rules gates and scheduled worker together before enabling households. Cloud Scheduler and the Functions service account need the deployment project's normal Firestore/Auth permissions. Scheduler production invocation and production index readiness remain staging checks; no deployment occurred here. The emulator does not automatically invoke schedules, so tests call the same service worker directly. Tests include queued service retries, concurrency, 520 memberships, recent authentication, last admin, single/multiple guardians, self unlink, cleanup hooks, stale-token rules/callables and Auth failure after provider success.

Watch pending job age, `attempts` and `lastError`. `CLEANUP_RETRY_REQUIRED` needs inspection of service permissions, indexes or provider availability; do not clear the deny marker to let the user retry. A trusted operator can call `resumeDeletion(uid)` from the built backend under service credentials. Retry is bounded and idempotent; no caller Auth session is needed. Completed jobs are durable denial/audit records, not a temporary cache. Tombstone/audit retention and participant pseudonymization require reviewed policy before a live rollout.

P2.06 schedule drafts and immutable draft revisions are organization-owned operational history, like teams/events. They preserve standard actor/audit metadata and division fixture labels under the same retention policy. Transient coach IDs and occupancy snapshots are not persisted. Existing `commandRequests.uid` cleanup removes draft receipts; the account tombstone immediately denies draft generation, reads, edits, validation, publication attempts and receipt replay. No new account-owned cleanup hook is required.

P2.20 adds an `attachmentAudit` stage after the other cleanup pages and before Auth deletion. It pages the `attachments` collection group on `ownerUid` and writes `residualAudit.attachments` on the job: the field name, the count, `anonymized: false`, and `bytesRetained: true`. The documents and objects stay. File names are not copied onto the job. Message `authorUid` is also left in place. Changing either retention choice needs the owner policy in `docs/phase-2-pilot-runbook.md`.
